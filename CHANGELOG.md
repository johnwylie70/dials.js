# Changes

## Unreleased

- Fixed: a select or rotary did not follow a choice changed in code when its target reads the choice back by name (`lead.wave = "sine"`). It now takes a place in the list, a name in any case, or `false` and `true` for two choices, and says nothing back to the target.
- The keyboard is the instrument and keeps its height. When the plates, the scope and the keyboard do not all fit, the scope is made shorter (no less than 56 points, and it is given back any height left over), and if that is not enough the plates are moved under a scope and a keyboard that stay put. Only where that would leave hardly any room to see the plates does the keyboard give up height, and only on a picture under 480 points tall (a phone on its side) is the scope put away, which is said once in the console. A height given to `keyboard` is kept to. On a panel with height to spare, the scope takes it first and then the keyboard, up to 190 points.
- A page inside another's frame no longer keeps the screen's safe edges clear (it was told of them though it was nowhere near them, which left an empty band at the top).
- A dial turned by ratio may start at 0: `describe()` giving `{ min: 0, log: true }` puts 0 at the bottom of the dial, and `logFrom` says where the turn starts above it. `labels: { 0: "auto" }` (from `describe()`, or as an option) shows a name in place of a value; `describe()` may also give `format(value)`.
- A rotary with its names round it no longer names its choice a second time underneath (`showValue: true` or `false` says otherwise), and its title and knob are on a line with the dials beside it.
- Moving the plates is what a panel does when there is more than fits: they carry on a little when a finger lets go, a drag that starts between plates passes over dials without turning them, a finger going up or down a slider that lies across moves the plates and one going along it moves the slider, a thin bar shows where they are (along the bottom too, when they move sideways), and `over(x, y)` is true everywhere they can be moved.
- `layout({ fit: "width" })` fills the width whichever way: rows as written, drawn smaller to fit or bigger where there is room, no bigger than `maxScale` (1.6) and no bigger than fits the height.
- A target can say that a setting has changed shape: `onChange(fn)`, and `fn(name)` when its choices or its range are not what they were. A select or rotary keeps its choice if it is still there, a dial or slider takes the new range, and the control is laid out again. A choice of `kind: "text"` is given to the target as the text it is.
- `rotary("mode", ["room", "hall", "plate", "spring"], "hall")`, or `rotary("wave", lead, "wave")`: a knob that clicks between a few choices, with a notch for each and their names round it where there is room. It holds at a notch and clicks over, goes to a tapped name or notch, and two taps put it back. `select(…, { style: "rotary" })` is the same.
- The piano keys slide to a new octave (the arrows, Z and X, an arrow held down, or a finger dragging the strip above the keys, which settles on a whole octave). A key pressed part way plays the note under the finger. They jump for reduced motion, or with `{ animate: false }`. A keyboard never shows so many octaves that a key is too narrow to play, and its arrows are bigger under a finger.
- Wrapping is yours to say: `layout({ wrap: false })` keeps every row as written and draws the whole panel at one scale that fits the widest to the width (not below `minScale`: by default eight tenths under a finger and half with a mouse, where rows are moved from side to side instead); `layout({ wrap: true })` makes every `row()` a new line; `layout({ columns: 3 })`, or `{ phone: 1, tablet: 2, wide: 3 }`, says how many plates to a line; `group("shape", { wrap: false }, …)` never splits; `row({ wrap: false }, …)` keeps the groups in it side by side; `wrap()` marks where a line may be split.
- `layout({ size: [900, 640] })`: a panel of a set size, laid out once and made to fit any screen whole, keeping its shape (`size: 900`, or `fit: "width"`, fills the width). It is what `design` was, and `design` still works.
- A size may be so many times the usual (`{ size: 1.5 }`), and is kept at every scale; dials of different sizes on a line have their labels and values on the same lines.
- `layout()` gives back `{ width, height, scale }`, and `layout({ onResize })` is called when they change.
- On a phone on its side the scope above the keyboard is put away and the keyboard keeps to its share of the height; a strip down one side folds into two columns when it is too tall; the screen's safe edges (a notch, a home bar) are kept clear.
- Layout. Controls are kept on titled plates: a `label` made just before some controls is their heading, and `group("filter", () => { … })` (or `group("filter")` and then the controls) says so with options. Plates wrap whole, so a heading is never left behind; wrapped lines are balanced; the plates of a line share its width and every line is as wide as the widest. Scenes written with `label` and `row()` alone are laid out this way with no change.
- Plates with titles fill the width: they stand side by side in balanced lines set against both edges of the picture, as many to a line as fit (one on a phone, two in a tablet's split view, three or four across a tablet), and a `row()` between them says where a line should end when one has to. A few controls with no titles keep their own width and their rows as written. On a scene that draws nothing of its own, the scope above the keyboard takes spare height, so there is no dead room between them.
- `row`, `column` and `grid(3, …)` arrange what is inside a group, and nest. `spacer()` takes the room left over. Any control takes `size: "small"` or `"large"`, `share` (its part of the room its line has left) and `span` (columns of a grid). A group takes `colour`, `columns`, `gap`, `align`, `dividers` and `plate`.
- Controls are drawn a little smaller before a line wraps, and never tiny (not under eight tenths, and on a touch screen not under their size with a mouse). The keyboard and the scope above it give up some height next. What still does not fit is moved with a finger, the wheel or a bar, while the keyboard and that scope stay put. Before, controls shrank to six tenths and then ran under the keyboard.
- `page("fx")`: pages of plates, turned with a tab.
- `group("moves", { at: "left" }, …)` is a strip along an edge, `layout({ at: "left" })` puts every plate down one side, and `room()` is the rectangle no control is on.
- `layout({ design: [800, 480] })`: `x` and `y` are in a panel of that size, scaled to the picture. A group can be placed too. `layout({ image, compact, dividers, plates, align })` for the look.
- `examples/front_panel.js`: a whole synth in seven groups. The stand-in synth has the settings for it (voices, detune, a sub, decay and sustain, an echo and a room).
- Changed: a label that comes before controls is drawn above them, as their plate's title, not at their left; a scene's rows of plates are all one width; a host's listener is also given `f`, the rectangle no control is on, when it changes.
- Dials, sliders (across or upright), buttons, toggles and selects: `dial("speed", 0, 10)` gives a number to read, and `dial("cutoff", lead, "filter_cutoff")` is that setting of `lead`, with its range, unit and starting value taken from it.
- A piano keyboard, `keyboard(lead, { octave: 4 })`: several fingers at once, sliding from key to key, the computer's keys by where they are (the row from A is the white keys), Z and X to step the octave, and keys lit while they sound.
- A scope, `scope(lead)`, for a sound's wave or any value over time (`graph.push(value)`), and a level meter, `meter(lead)`.
- Controls lay themselves out in rows, with the keyboard along the bottom, and are laid out again when the picture changes size. `{ x, y }` places one by hand; `row()` starts a new row; `layout({ at: "bottom" })` moves the rows.
- A drag that leaves a control, or the picture, keeps turning it. Shift turns a tenth as far, and so does pulling well away from a dial (a quarter). Two taps put a dial or slider back where it started.
- They are drawn on a canvas of their own over the scene's, so they sit on 2D and 3D scenes alike and need no call in `draw()`.
- Four examples: a dial that shapes a star, a synth panel, a keyboard with a scope, and a bouncing ball with no sound at all.
- More controls, for any program. `number("lives", 0, 9, 3)` is a number box: − and + to step (hold one to keep stepping), a drag up or down on the number, or a tap on it to type. `textBox("name", "Ada")` is a line of text that is typed, with `onChange` and `onEnter`. `readout("score", () => score)` shows a value or a message from code, with `unit`, `digits`, `size: "large"` and `format: "clock"`; its digits each take the same room, so a number that is counting does not shake.
- Typing uses a real box for text laid over the control for as long as someone types (the device's own keyboard, caret and paste), made as the tap ends so that a phone brings its keyboard up, with letters of 16 pixels or more so that the page is not zoomed. It keeps to its control when the picture is scaled or the page scrolled, works in a frame that may only run scripts, and what is typed does not reach the scene's `keys`.
- The control touched last takes the arrow keys, and Tab goes to the next that takes keys; a ring shows which once a key has been used.
- `checkbox("sound", true)`, a toggle drawn as a box that is ticked; `choose("mode", ["easy", "medium", "hard"])`, one of a few side by side (a finger sliding across them chooses as it goes; on a narrow screen they take two lines); `radio(…)`, the same one under another; and `dropdown("planet", planets)`, whose list opens over the controls, is moved with a finger or the wheel when it is long, and closes on a pick or a touch outside it, which is not felt by what is underneath. `over(x, y)` is true everywhere while a list is open.
- `xyPad("filter", lead, "cutoff", "resonance")`, a square where one finger sets two numbers (or two plain ranges; `spring: true` lets it go back). Its numbers are `pad.x` and `pad.y`: on the control itself those names say where it is drawn, so a scene is handed something that stands for it, with the control as its `control`.
- `colourPicker("ink", "tomato")`, a patch that opens a picker (a square for how strong and how bright, a strip of hues, sixteen named colours); its value is a colour as a scene writes one. `progress("loading", 0.4)`, `light("ready", true)` (on or off, a brightness, a colour, or as bright as a target is loud) and `busy("working")`.
- `rangeSlider("price", 0, 100, [20, 80])`, a slider with a low and a high handle that never pass each other, and `fader(…)`, an upright slider with the look of a mixing desk's.
- For games. `joystick()`: a stick that springs back, read as `stick.x` and `stick.y` (each -1 to 1, in the scene's own directions), `stick.angle` and `stick.distance`; `floating: true` puts it where the thumb lands. `dpad()`: a pad of arrows, read as `pad.left`, `pad.right`, `pad.up`, `pad.down`, `pad.x` and `pad.y`. `actionButton("A")`: a round button. Each finger holds its own control.
- `dpad({ asKeys: true })` and `actionButton("jump", { key: "space" })` hold keys down for the scene, by sending its canvas what a real key sends: a game written for `keys.has("ArrowLeft")` is played by touch unchanged. Such a key is the scene's alone: a control that has the arrow keys does not take it.
- Two examples: `settings_panel.js` (a name, a number, a choice, a colour and a tick box that change a drawing) and `joystick_game.js` (a ship flown with the stick, a button to fire, and the arrow keys and space bar doing the same).
- For music. `envelope("shape", lead)`: a note's attack, decay, sustain and release as a graph whose corners are dragged, found on the target by those names (or given the four names, or used alone). `stepGrid("drums", 16, ["kick", "snare", "hat"])`: a grid of steps on or off, filled by tapping and by dragging along, read with `grid.get("kick", 0)` and `grid.value`, with `grid.step` lighting the column that is playing; on a narrow screen a long row takes two lines. `pads("kit", ["kick", "snare"], drums)`: pads hit several at once, which play a target's notes harder the nearer their middle. `pitchWheel` (rolls back) and `modWheel` (stays).
- `examples/drum_machine.js`: a grid, pads, a tempo and the shape of each hit, with the stand-in synth.
- Patterns. `gatePattern("gate", pad)`: a gate's steps as bars, tapped on and off, set by how high the finger is and drawn across, with a − and + for how many. `arpPattern("arp", lead)`: an arpeggio as a grid, steps across and chord notes up the side, a cell tapped for a note and again for a rest, dragged sideways to hold it on, with a lane of accents (and of slides) and named patterns. `steps("accent", 16)`: the lane of steps both are made of. Each is a list and is text (`"x.x.x5x.X..."`, `"1 - 3 2 _ 1! 3~ 5^"`), and either may be set; the marks are two small tables. Tied to a target, a pattern is one setting, found by name, kept as text or a list as the target keeps it, lit at the step the target's `step()` says, with the target's rate, depth and the rest beside it on one plate.
- `drumSeq("beat", ["kick", "snare", "hat", "clap"], drums)`: a drum sequencer that plays by itself, with play and stop, tempo (and tap tempo), swing, starter patterns and clear on the widget, accents by dragging up, a mute and a listen for each row, and pages of steps on a narrow screen. A target with `time()` is handed each step ahead of time, with the time it is to sound.
- The grid of steps is made of the same lanes, and has `text`. `examples/drum_machine.js` is now a `drumSeq` with pads; `examples/trance.js` is a gated chord and an arpeggio.
