# Changes

## Unreleased

- `rotary("mode", ["room", "hall", "plate", "spring"], "hall")`, or `rotary("wave", lead, "wave")`: a knob that clicks between a few choices, with a notch for each and their names round it where there is room. It holds at a notch and clicks over, goes to a tapped name or notch, and two taps put it back. `select(…, { style: "rotary" })` is the same.
- The piano keys slide to a new octave (the arrows, Z and X, an arrow held down, or a finger dragging the strip above the keys, which settles on a whole octave). A key pressed part way plays the note under the finger. They jump for reduced motion, or with `{ animate: false }`. A keyboard never shows so many octaves that a key is too narrow to play, and its arrows are bigger under a finger.
- Wrapping is yours to say: `layout({ wrap: false })` keeps every row as written and draws the whole panel at one scale that fits the widest to the width (not below `minScale`: by default eight tenths under a finger and half with a mouse, where rows are moved from side to side instead); `layout({ wrap: true })` makes every `row()` a new line; `layout({ columns: 3 })`, or `{ phone: 1, tablet: 2, wide: 3 }`, says how many plates to a line; `group("shape", { wrap: false }, …)` never splits; `row({ wrap: false }, …)` keeps the groups in it side by side; `wrap()` marks where a line may be split.
- `layout({ size: [900, 640] })`: a panel of a set size, laid out once and made to fit any screen whole, keeping its shape (`size: 900`, or `fit: "width"`, fills the width). It is what `design` was, and `design` still works.
- A size may be so many times the usual (`{ size: 1.5 }`), and is kept at every scale; dials of different sizes on a line have their labels and values on the same lines.
- `layout()` gives back `{ width, height, scale }`, and `layout({ onResize })` is called when they change.
- On a phone on its side the scope above the keyboard is put away and the keyboard keeps to its share of the height; a strip down one side folds into two columns when it is too tall; the screen's safe edges (a notch, a home bar) are kept clear.
- Layout. Controls are kept on titled plates: a `label` made just before some controls is their heading, and `group("filter", () => { … })` (or `group("filter")` and then the controls) says so with options. Plates wrap whole, so a heading is never left behind; wrapped lines are balanced; the plates of a line share its width and every line is as wide as the widest. Scenes written with `label` and `row()` alone are laid out this way with no change.
- Plates with titles fill the width: they stand side by side in balanced lines set against both edges of the picture, as many to a line as fit (one on a phone, two in a tablet's split view, three or four across a tablet), and a `row()` between them says where a line should end when one has to. A few controls with no titles keep their own width and their rows as written. On a scene that draws nothing of its own, the scope above the keyboard takes spare height, so there is no dead room between them.
- `row`, `column` and `grid(3, …)` arrange what is inside a group, and nest. `spacer()` takes the room left over. Any control takes `size: "small"` or `"large"`, `share` (its part of the room its line has left) and `span` (columns of a grid). A group takes `colour`, `columns`, `gap`, `align`, `dividers` and `plate`.
- Controls are drawn a little smaller before a line wraps, and never tiny (not under eight tenths, and on a touch screen not under their size with a mouse). The keyboard and the scope above it give up some height next. What still does not fit is moved with a finger, the wheel or a bar, while the keyboard and that scope stay put. Before, controls shrank to six tenths and then ran under the keyboard.
- `page("fx")`: pages of plates, turned with a tab.
- `group("moves", { at: "left" }, …)` is a strip along an edge, `layout({ at: "left" })` puts every plate down one side, and `room()` is the rectangle no control is on.
- `layout({ design: [800, 480] })`: `x` and `y` are in a panel of that size, scaled to the picture. A group can be placed too. `layout({ image, compact, dividers, plates, align })` for the look.
- `examples/front_panel.js`: a whole synth in seven groups. The stand-in synth has the settings for it (voices, detune, a sub, decay and sustain, an echo and a room).
- Changed: a label that comes before controls is drawn above them, as their plate's title, not at their left; a scene's rows of plates are all one width; a host's listener is also given `f`, the rectangle no control is on, when it changes.
- Dials, sliders (across or upright), buttons, toggles and selects: `dial("speed", 0, 10)` gives a number to read, and `dial("cutoff", lead, "filter_cutoff")` is that setting of `lead`, with its range, unit and starting value taken from it.
- A piano keyboard, `keyboard(lead, { octave: 4 })`: several fingers at once, sliding from key to key, the computer's keys by where they are (the row from A is the white keys), Z and X to step the octave, and keys lit while they sound.
- A scope, `scope(lead)`, for a sound's wave or any value over time (`graph.push(value)`), and a level meter, `meter(lead)`.
- Controls lay themselves out in rows, with the keyboard along the bottom, and are laid out again when the picture changes size. `{ x, y }` places one by hand; `row()` starts a new row; `layout({ at: "bottom" })` moves the rows.
- A drag that leaves a control, or the picture, keeps turning it. Shift turns a tenth as far, and so does pulling well away from a dial (a quarter). Two taps put a dial or slider back where it started.
- They are drawn on a canvas of their own over the scene's, so they sit on 2D and 3D scenes alike and need no call in `draw()`.
- Four examples: a dial that shapes a star, a synth panel, a keyboard with a scope, and a bouncing ball with no sound at all.
